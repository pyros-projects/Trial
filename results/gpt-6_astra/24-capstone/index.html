About this idea

The quiet logic of cloth.

A fabric is a conversation between two sets of threads: lengthwise warp and crosswise weft. At each crossing, one goes over. A small repeat can become an entire surface.

Selvedge lets you change that conversation. Paint crossings, turn the cloth over, follow a thread, and find long floats—stretches that travel on one side before binding again. A run can cross the edge of the repeat. A thread that never changes sides is marked unbound.

Why a pocket loom?

We wanted a tactile way to discover structure hidden in everyday fabric. Nearby tools such as WeaveWorld plan threading and lifting; AdaCAD connects drafting operations. Here, you work directly with crossings and their two faces. Unlike a graphics editor or a deformable-cloth simulation, the subject is how cloth is interlaced.

What the controls mean

Reverse turns the cloth left to right and swaps the visible yarn. The small repeat editor always shows the front. Bind one float tests single crossing changes and chooses a local improvement, prioritizing fewer unbound threads, then shorter excess floats. It may reach a point where several coordinated edits are needed. Repeat size tiles or crops your existing crossings. Yarn rhythm repeats independently of crossings. The editor and outlined repeat show the same first set of yarn colors. Reset restores the original diamond study, colors, views and empty history, and cancels pending file work. Reloading does the same.
