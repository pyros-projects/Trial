A field guide to your atmosphere
Small interventions. Emergent weather.
01 · Follow the water
Water evaporates from lakes, ocean, and wet ground. Wind transports this vapor. Rising air cools; vapor condenses into cloud water, releasing heat. Heavy clouds form precipitation, which falls, partly evaporates, and wets the ground.
02 · Shape a storm
Select Heat and paint the landscape. Add Moisture in the same area, then give it time: uplift and cooling turn excess vapor into cloud. The Wind and Pressure tools redirect this response. Brush radius and strength work continuously as you paint.
03 · Read the fields
The Field selector reveals eleven views of the actual atmosphere. The Map samples your selected altitude; Section cuts west to east through the probe. Turn on vectors in Settings to see wind directions and streamlines. Precipitation rate is sedimentation flux in mm/h; cloud and rain water are measured in g/kg.
04 · Put down a probe
Choose the crosshair tool and click the terrain or map. Telemetry samples your selected altitude. Inspect opens a vertical profile with temperature, vapor, cloud, rain, pressure, and wind. Each probe has a time series you can export as CSV.
Move through the scene
Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, and Shift + drag or right-drag to pan. The camera button cycles cinematic views. The eye enables fly-through: drag to look, W A S D to move, Q E down/up. Esc leaves fly mode. Touch supports single-finger painting and two-finger pan/zoom.
Experiment with confidence
Space pauses; . advances one step. Reset restores the active preset and seed. Lightning responds to a storm charge proxy, or use the bolt to inspect a strike. Enable thunder with the audio checkbox. Save and load preserve the complete deterministic atmosphere.
This is an educational, accelerated model with coarse spatial resolution, approximate buoyancy and rotation, and simplified microphysics. Diagnostic units describe model state; it is not a forecast or a quantitatively validated weather model.
