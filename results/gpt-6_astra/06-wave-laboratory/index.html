Build an experiment

Pick a source, then click the field to place it. Select and drag emitters, probes, or structures to move them. Barrier, medium, absorber, and eraser tools paint continuously. Lens and obstacle tools grow a shape as you drag; slit draws a wall with an opening. A click with Phased array places eight independently editable elements.

Read the field

Amplitude shows signed displacement: cool tones are negative, warm tones positive. Intensity is exponentially averaged u², in arbitrary units. Phase uses atan2(−v / 2πf, u) relative to the selected source frequency; it is a local harmonic estimate and is unreliable at nodes or for mixed frequencies. Gradient shows |∇u|. Medium shows refractive index. Energy flow draws arrows along −v∇u.

Listen with your eyes

Probes sample the actual local field after each numerical step. Each shows an auto-scaled scrolling waveform, peak amplitude, and a dominant-frequency estimate from a windowed spectrum of the sampled field. The spectral estimate resolves up to 40 Hz, limited by the sampling rate, and uses up to 15 seconds of history for slow waves; the waveform displays the last five seconds. Wait for several cycles; “—” means there is insufficient signal. Clear field also clears measurement history.

The physics underneath

uₜₜ + γuₜ = c(x,y)² ∇²u + sources
c(x,y) = c₀ / n(x,y)   ·   CFL ≤ 0.92

A finite-difference grid evolves displacement and velocity. Barriers impose zero displacement and reflect with phase inversion. Absorbers damp the field; open boundaries use a gradual sponge, so a small residual reflection is possible. Square and triangle emitters use a few harmonics to limit grid aliasing. High frequencies need finer grids. Changing resolution clears the field. The timestep is automatically capped before a step to satisfy the 2D stability limit.
