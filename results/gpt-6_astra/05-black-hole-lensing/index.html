Field notes
A SMALL GUIDE TO A VERY LARGE IDEA
Follow the light
The dark region is a shadow: rays traced backward from your eye fall into the event horizon. Nearby rays can loop around the mass before escaping. The bright arch is light from the far side of the accretion disk, bent over the black hole toward you.
A model you can look inside
Horizon integrates spatial null-geodesic trajectories with a velocity-Verlet method and smaller steps near the mass. In reference units, the non-spinning central acceleration is:
x″ = −1.5 · rₛ · |x × v|² · x / |x|⁵
The mass control scales rₛ; horizon size independently adjusts the absorption boundary. At the standard boundary, the Schwarzschild photon sphere is at 1.5 rₛ. Spin adds an illustrative transverse force; this is not a full Kerr spacetime solver.
Why the disk glows
Each curved ray intersects a finite-thickness annular volume. Emission varies with radius, temperature, and advected procedural turbulence. Gravitational redshift and orbital Doppler factors shift its spectrum and brightness. The approaching side is brighter. Bloom is a screen-space scattering approximation.
Reading a ray
Click any pixel. The inspector repeats the same integration on the CPU and shows its projected trajectory, closest approach, bending, first disk hit, and energy drift. Tiny CPU/GPU differences near the unstable photon orbit are expected. Deflection is accumulated tangent turning, so it can exceed 180°. Orange in the classification view means the step budget was exhausted; magenta indicates numerical failure.
Reference
Spatial central-force formulation: Belbruno & Pretorius, A Dynamical Systems Approach to Schwarzschild Null Geodesics, equation 12, arXiv:1103.0585 (2011). The observer projection and emission are approximations intended for exploration, not precision astrophysics.
