Behind the experiment

About this idea

A memory is more than the surface it lives on. Afterimage lets you make a tiny drawing, take pieces of its encoded form away, and find out what survives.

Why this exists

Error-correcting codes usually live out of sight. Here, loss has a shape. A fold that destroys whole bands in Rows can survive in Woven, because the same missing tiles are spread among more bands. This is a creative experiment in resilience, built around a real byte codec.

The mechanism

Each of 24 bands holds 24 image bytes plus 4, 8, or 12 redundant bytes. A systematic Reed–Solomon code over GF(256) evaluates a polynomial through the data points. Any 24 surviving points reconstruct it exactly. The decoder receives only surviving bytes and the chosen recovery size. The original is retained separately for comparison. Woven is a simple interleaving of those code bands.

Its own opening

sigh’s interactive Reed–Solomon demo explores a text message and its polynomial steps. Afterimage asks a spatial question through editable drawings, scratches, controlled folds, and partial recovery. Its purpose is information loss and reconstruction; it is distinct from a texture graph editor or a circuit simulator. This is a contribution to this collection, not a claim of worldwide originality.

Real limits

This is a small model of known-location erasures, not correction of arbitrary wrong bytes. More than the recovery budget in one band leaves that band underdetermined; missing data remains hatched instead of guessed. The picture has 24 × 24 pixels and eight inks. It is not archival software, encryption, or a QR/CD implementation. All changes stay in memory. Reset session restores the initial moth, choices, and empty history, and invalidates pending imports. Reload does the same.
