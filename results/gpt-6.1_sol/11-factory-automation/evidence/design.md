# FERRO — factory automation game

The requested outcome is a complete offline game delivered in `index.html`, validated with real browser interactions at desktop and narrow sizes. The user explicitly requested one continuous implementation run; design and plan approval pauses are superseded by that instruction.

Use a canvas grid and a fixed 50 ms simulation. Individual transport packets advance through capacity-limited belts; machines maintain input/output buffers and consume exact recipes. Transfers are separate from movement so a packet cannot move twice in one tick. Splitters alternate available ports, mergers accept three input sides, and inserters pull from the structure behind them. Deposits are finite. Coal powers generators; generators and nearby poles form components, consumers attach to nearby nodes, and component brownouts scale real work.

Provide extractors, belts, splitters, mergers, inserters, furnaces, presses, assemblers, storage, generators, power poles, delivery docks, and obstacles. Include iron/copper plates, gears, wire, steel, circuits, and motors. Circuits combine wire and iron; motors combine circuits and gears. Construction actions have costs in campaign mode. Three escalating contracts score elapsed time, energy, and footprint; sandbox has unlimited construction. Six deterministic curated factories demonstrate normal production, congestion, power shortages, multiple products, and many packets.

The interface uses a charcoal industrial palette, amber active tools, colored resource identities, technical numerals, a left construction rail, central animated canvas, right inspection/analytics panel, and bottom simulation controls. It has canvas zoom/pan, selection, area deletion, rotation, copy and move, route-aware belt drawing, power drawing, placement feedback, undo/redo, overlays, tooltips, history charts, and generated interface sounds after a gesture. On narrow screens the canvas remains primary and the tools/inspector become compact drawers.
