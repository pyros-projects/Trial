You are an autonomous research, writing, motion-design, audio-design, and video-production agent.

Your task is to research, design, produce, review, improve, and deliver a complete short documentary/explainer video about the trajectory of AI progress and what that trajectory could mean for the world.

Do not ask me how to approach it. Make the creative and technical decisions yourself.

## THE QUESTION

**If the pace of AI progress observed over the last several years does not slow down, what does AI look like in 6 months, 1 year, 2 years, 5 years, and 10 years - and what does the world itself look like as a consequence?**

The premise of the piece is deliberately aggressive:

Assume that the underlying rate of progress does **not** plateau or meaningfully slow down, and that the major exponential or compounding trends observed so far continue.

Treat this as a conditional scenario:

> "What follows if the curve keeps going?"

Do not quietly replace this premise with a conservative forecast.

At the same time, distinguish clearly between:

- things that have already happened
- measurable current trends
- extrapolations from those trends
- first-order consequences
- second-order societal and economic consequences
- increasingly speculative downstream consequences

The result should feel intellectually serious rather than like AI hype.

# 2. FORECAST AI AND THE WORLD AROUND IT

Using the researched trajectory, extrapolate forward under the stated assumption that progress continues at approximately the same exponential/compounding pace.

Cover these horizons explicitly:

- **6 months**
- **1 year**
- **2 years**
- **5 years**
- **10 years**

Anchor each horizon to an actual calendar date based on today's date.

For every horizon, answer **two connected questions**:

### A. What can AI do?

Consider things such as:

- capability
- reasoning
- autonomous task duration
- reliability
- multimodality
- agent coordination
- software engineering
- scientific research
- robotics
- computer use
- cost of intelligence
- inference speed
- ability to learn from experience
- AI-assisted AI research
- number of useful parallel agents one person or organization can operate

### B. What does a world containing systems like this look like?

Do not stop at describing better models.

Follow their capabilities outward into the real world.

Explore plausible consequences for areas such as:

- software and technology
- companies and organizational structure
- productivity
- employment and the nature of human work
- entrepreneurship and the size of teams required to build things
- scientific discovery
- medicine and biotechnology
- education
- media and entertainment
- creative work
- manufacturing
- robotics and physical automation
- consumer products
- personal AI assistants
- economics
- markets and competition
- energy and infrastructure
- government and institutions
- cybersecurity
- defense and geopolitical competition where relevant
- the speed at which new technologies diffuse through society
- everyday life

Look for **second-order effects**, not just obvious first-order automation.

For example:

If software becomes dramatically cheaper to create, do not merely say "programmers become more productive."

Ask what happens when software that previously required a 100-person company can be created by five people, one person, or eventually an automated organization.

If scientific research accelerates, ask what happens when research itself becomes an input into further AI progress.

If millions of competent agents become cheap, ask what organizations built around effectively unlimited digital labor might look like.

The further into the future the film goes, the more the focus should naturally expand:

**AI capability -> organizations -> industries -> economy -> society -> the texture of everyday life.**

Do not simply invent benchmark scores for the distant future.

Instead ask:

**What does another N months or years of this rate of progress imply?**

Translate abstract curves into consequences people can intuitively understand.

For longer horizons, ordinary forecasting language itself may start becoming strained. If so, make that difficulty part of the story.

Do not manufacture certainty where none exists, but honor the premise of continued exponential progress.

# 3. MODEL COMPOUNDING EFFECTS

Do not treat different AI trends as independent.

Pay particular attention to feedback loops such as:

**better AI**
-> better AI-assisted research  
-> faster algorithmic progress  
-> better AI

**better AI**
-> cheaper software creation  
-> more experimentation  
-> more products and infrastructure  
-> more data and automation  
-> better AI-enabled systems

**cheaper intelligence**
-> more agents  
-> more parallel work  
-> faster iteration  
-> lower cost  
-> even more deployment

The most important consequences may emerge from interactions between trends rather than any single capability milestone.

Identify those interactions.

Make them understandable visually.

# 4. TURN THE RESEARCH INTO A STORY

Create a tightly written narrative designed for a video with a **recommended duration of approximately 5 minutes** and an **absolute maximum duration of 10 minutes**.

Aim for roughly **7:00** if the story works at that length.

However, do **not** force the film into five minutes if doing so makes the later sections rushed, superficial, or difficult to understand.

The additional time up to the seven-minute ceiling should primarily be used when needed to properly explore how AI capability changes propagate into organizations, industries, society, and everyday life.

Do not use the larger time budget as an excuse for repetition, slow pacing, or filler.

The film should remain dense, deliberate, and tightly edited.

It should not feel like a PowerPoint presentation with narration.

Build an actual visual story.

A possible narrative shape is:

1. Establish what "five years" used to mean in AI.
2. Compress the last five years visually.
3. Zoom aggressively into the last twelve months.
4. Reveal that the distance between capability milestones is shrinking.
5. Ask what happens if we simply extend the trajectory.
6. Move through +6 months and +1 year, where the effects remain recognizably connected to today's world.
7. At +2 years, show capabilities beginning to reshape workflows, organizations, science, and industries.
8. At +5 years, zoom out from AI systems toward the society constructed around them.
9. At +10 years, stop asking only "What can the AI do?" and instead ask "What kind of civilization exists when intelligence itself is abundant?"
10. Finish on one memorable visual or conceptual idea that reframes how the viewer thinks about exponential progress.

You are free to invent a better structure.

The opening 10-15 seconds must immediately earn attention.

Avoid a generic "Artificial intelligence is changing the world..." introduction.

The video should progressively **zoom outward in scope**.

Early:

**models**

Then:

**agents and humans**

Then:

**companies and laboratories**

Then:

**industries**

Then:

**the world**

### Musical identity

If music is useful, prefer an **original or appropriately licensed/generated musical treatment** rather than a generic stock track running continuously underneath the narration.

For example, the motif itself could become:

- faster
- denser
- more layered
- more synthetic
- more expansive
- increasingly difficult to distinguish from the surrounding sound design

if that serves the narrative.

Do not over-score the film.

Silence is also a creative tool.

If a major idea benefits from removing the music completely, do so.

### Synchronization

Audio and visuals should feel authored together.

Important visual events should often have corresponding audio events.

Narration timing should account for visual comprehension.

Do not fire dense narration over a chart before the viewer has time to understand the chart.

Use pauses intentionally.

Cuts, transitions, animation beats, data reveals, narration, sound effects, and music should feel rhythmically coordinated.

### Mix quality

Produce a polished final mix.

Ensure:

- narration remains intelligible at all times
- music does not compete with speech
- sound effects are not excessively loud
- transitions do not create sudden uncontrolled volume jumps
- the soundtrack has appropriate dynamic range
- there is no clipping
- the final loudness is appropriate for online video

The soundtrack should still make sense if heard without watching the screen, just as important visual sequences should remain understandable without audio.
